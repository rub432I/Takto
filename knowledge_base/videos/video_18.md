## 📝 Transcript Content & Summary

To get professional-quality design from AI, you need to shift from simple prompting to a structured design process. According to Leon Lin, the secret isn't a long prompt, but rather setting clear constraints and iterative feedback loops.

Key steps for better AI design:

Use strong visual references: Instead of describing your design, provide 9–12 high-quality screenshots from sites like Dribbble or Awwwards (0:53-1:36). This helps the AI understand layout, spacing, and typography better than words can (1:16-1:36).
Create a target mockup first: Don't build the full app immediately. Ask your agent to build a simple web-based mockup first (4:09-4:34). This allows you to test styles and layout quickly without burning through tokens.
Set a specific bar: Assign a numerical goal for quality (e.g., aiming for 99.9% accuracy to a reference) to force the AI to keep iterating rather than stopping as soon as the page is functional (5:03-6:32).
Ban the 'slop': AI designs often include repetitive patterns (beige backgrounds, generic fonts). Maintain a list of forbidden design elements to force the model to try new, more intentional choices (8:48-10:14).
Iterate step-by-step: Separate your build process. Focus on the static design first, then add animations later using reference videos to guide movement (10:15-11:11).
Use a separate judge: The AI that builds the design is often too biased to evaluate it fairly. Use a second agent (a different model or a fresh session) to score the work and provide feedback (11:53-12:53).

If you want to automate this workflow, you can use the Cook skill, which loops these steps until your design meets your specified quality bar (12:53-15:08).
