## 📝 Transcript Content & Summary

The video identifies six common habits of "AI slop" and explains how the ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English standard systematically eliminates them (5:53 - 6:53):

1.  Synonym Rotation (Rule 1.11): Avoid using multiple names for the same thing; use one name per object and the approved dictionary.
2.  Hedging (Rule 3.4): Remove complex verb constructions and filler phrasing that lacks substance.
3.  Frozen Verbs (Rule 3.7): Use verbs for actions instead of turning them into nouns (e.g., use "analyze" instead of "perform an analysis").
4.  Marketing Adjectives (Dictionary constraint): Marketing terms like "seamless," "robust," or "cutting-edge" are excluded from the approved 900-word dictionary.
5.  Run-on Sentences (Rules 5.1 & 6.3): Adhere to hard length caps (max 20 words for instructions, 25 for descriptions) and ban semicolons.
6.  Phrasal Verbs (Rule 9.3): Avoid chatty phrasal verbs (like "take off") and use direct, approved verbs (like "remove").
