## 📝 Transcript Content & Summary

To stop "vibe coding"—the practice of letting AI generate software that lacks intentionality and purpose—you must shift from simply prompting features to forming a clear product opinion. Kole Jain suggests the following strategies to move beyond surface-level imitation:

Design for the User, Not the Average: Instead of asking AI to create a component based on how it looks elsewhere, define exactly why it exists. Ask: "What would someone expect when they open this?" and tailor the options and styling to that specific need rather than mimicking common patterns (2:19 - 2:48).
Apply Tesler’s Law: Complexity cannot be removed, only relocated. Good design places the burden of complexity on the system (the creator) to keep the user's experience simple. Don't let AI default to making things easier for you at the expense of the user (3:34 - 3:45).
Prioritize Purposeful Onboarding: Avoid generic modal-based onboarding. Instead, build flows that ask users about their specific goals and then tailor the workspace to them. A good onboarding sequence should build the product around the user's needs (4:26 - 4:54).
Create Interconnected Experiences: Vibe-coded apps often create separate pages that don't talk to each other. Take inspiration from Wikipedia by using links and hover-previews to keep users in their flow, allowing them to jump between context without leaving their primary task (6:23 - 7:14).
Develop an Opinion: AI, by default, provides an average output. To create a great product, you must have a strong design perspective—even one that might be controversial or unconventional—much like Apple did with the Magic Mouse (7:39 - 8:22).
